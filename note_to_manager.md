**Subject: Applications Jul–Sep 2026: three answers before planning**

**1. Best source.** Referrals bring the most hires: 10.7 per 100 applications (13 of 121), then email 8.1, search 5.7, college 3.4, social 2.3. It does not hold for every role. Referral leads Design and Marketing, but email leads Software, search leads Data and Finance, and college leads Operations. Those cells hold only 0–6 hires each, so treat them as noise. Referral versus email is not a proven gap; social being weakest is the firmest finding.

**2. Finishing all 4 steps.** Ys. 48% of finishers were shortlisted versus 9% of everyone else, a 39-point gap (95% interval 34–44), seen in every role. Finishing 3 steps gives only 13%, so the final step is what matters. This is correlation, not proof that finishing causes shortlisting.

**3. Paid vs unpaid replies.** Paid roles get a first reply in a median 3 days, unpaid in 7. 13% of paid applications never hear back versus 30% of unpaid. The gap holds in every role, work mode and month, so it is worth acting on.

**Recommendation:** nudge employers of unpaid roles to reply within 3 days, and test an easier step 4.

**Don't fully trust:** only 64 hires in total; 6 duplicate rows removed; 15 blank sources excluded from Q1; blank replies may include recent applicants.
